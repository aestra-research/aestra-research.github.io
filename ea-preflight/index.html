Psychdelic Preflight Checklist

Twelve questions to ask yourself before a psychedelic journey

If pilots can run a checklist, so can we. Mid-flight whoopsies can be tricky to handle.

Preparation is more than set and setting. In the four weeks before a journey, we can slow down, tune in and reflect.

The twelve conditions come from Alfried Längle's Existential Analysis. His account is that a person needs four things in order to say yes to their own existence, and that each of the four rests on three conditions. Twelve in all. They were being asked in consulting rooms for decades before anyone pointed them at psychedelics.

Four moments each week

  1. Tune in. Listen to the meditation for that Fundamental Motivation. Nothing to answer yet.
  2. Sit or lay. When the meditation is over, give yourself two or three minutes with nothing to do. This is part of it.
  3. Reflect. Three questions, one per existential need. Best to write by hand if you can; an audio note works too.
  4. Leave space. Come back to the page later in the week and add what surfaced. The first answer is rarely the whole answer.

If a question comes up empty

Some of these questions may come up empty, and that is worth as much as a full page. Perhaps more. An empty line is telling you something, and it does not require you to do anything about it today. You can just sit with it for now. And if several come up empty at once, or if what surfaces is more than you want to hold on your own, I encourage you to take it to someone before you take it into a journey: a therapist, a doctor, or a person who knows you well.

What this is not

  • Not a test, and not a score. Nothing here adds up to a number.
  • Not a diagnosis. It describes what you can say today, not what is true of you.
  • Not a clearance to proceed, and not medical advice.
  • Not a substitute for a therapist, a doctor, or a person who knows you.
  • Not an encouragement to take anything. What you do is your own decision, and the law where you live is your own responsibility.

With thanks

This work would not exist without the knowledge keepers who carried these medicines: María Sabina Magdalena García and the Mazatec people of Huautla de Jiménez above all, whose knowledge reached the West at a cost to her that she did not consent to and did not recover from. Elder Albert Marshall's Etuaptmumk, Two-Eyed Seeing, is the practice this page hopes to honour: the strength of Indigenous knowledge in one eye, the strength of Western knowledge in the other, and the skill to use both together.
